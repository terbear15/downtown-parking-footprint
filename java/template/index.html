Method

How the numbers are made

Downtown boundary (one rule for every city)

Downtown is where jobs are most concentrated. Jobs per census block come from the U.S. Census Bureau's LEHD Origin-Destination Employment Statistics (LODES 8, workplace area characteristics, all jobs, 2023; Detroit uses 2021, the latest year published for Michigan). Each block's jobs are assigned to a 250 m grid cell. A cell's density is the mean of its 3 × 3 neighbourhood in jobs per acre.

Downtown is the 4-connected group of cells with at least 25 jobs per acre that contains the densest cell within 3 km of city hall. Enclosed gaps are filled. The densest cell is the downtown core used in Question 2.

Exception: New York uses all of Manhattan (New York County): every 250 m cell that contains a 2020 census block centroid in the borough. Its core is set at Grand Central (Midtown).

Question 1 · Footprint (measurement)

  • Surface lots: OpenStreetMap areas tagged amenity=parking whose parking tag is surface or missing. Multi-storey, underground, rooftop, garage and street-side parking are excluded.
  • Parking area: the part of each lot inside the downtown boundary, clipped cell by cell.
  • Share: parking area ÷ downtown area. Downtown area is the number of cells × 250 m × 250 m and includes streets, parks and some water at the edges.
  • Size groups use each lot's full area: under 0.5 acre, 0.5–2 acres, over 2 acres.
  • Units: 1 acre = 43,560 sq ft = 4,046.86 m². Areas are computed in a local flat projection per city.

Limitations

What these numbers cannot tell you

  • Lower bound. Only lots that volunteers have mapped in OpenStreetMap are counted. Unmapped lots are missing, so real parking area is likely higher. Coverage differs by city. The next phase trains a segmentation model on aerial imagery to find unmapped lots.
  • Denominator. Downtown area includes streets, parks and, at the edges, some water, so the share is of all downtown land, not of buildable parcels.
  • Boundary rule. A different job-density threshold or cell size would give different boundaries. Manhattan is a special case and is not directly comparable with the other cities.
  • Lots crossing the boundary count only the area inside it. Size groups and the size criterion use the full lot.
  • Transit. Bus stops are dense downtown, so the transit criterion passes for most lots. Distance to rail is shown separately.
  • Building coverage depends on how completely OSM maps buildings in each city.
  • Not verified: zoning, ownership, contamination, historic designation, leases and whether a lot serves a nearby use. A candidate is not a feasible site.
  • No occupancy. Whether a lot is full or empty cannot be read from OSM or aerial imagery, so it is not shown.

About

Downtown Parking Footprint

American downtowns devote a large amount of central land to surface parking. This project measures that land in major U.S. downtowns and screens individual lots as candidates for future redevelopment, to give city building a direction backed by evidence.

It is a course research project in urban spatial analytics. Next steps: a U-Net segmentation model trained on NAIP aerial imagery to find lots missing from OpenStreetMap, and zoning data read with a language model to check the fifth criterion.
